## Growth is the beginning of the question

Higher revenue may look like good news. On its own, however, it does not explain how much of that growth remains after costs. Understanding performance requires looking at both.

This example uses an **entirely synthetic dataset** covering six months and two sales channels: online and a physical store. These are not client data or professional results.

## What this example shows

Across both channels, revenue goes from BRL 80,000 in January to BRL 138,000 in June: **72.5% growth**. Over the same period, costs rise from BRL 48,000 to BRL 102,000.

The simplified margin drops from **40% to approximately 26.1%**, a decline of 13.9 percentage points. The result in currency terms increases from BRL 32,000 to BRL 36,000, but grows more slowly than revenue.

There is no contradiction. Revenue, the result after costs and margin answer different questions.

## How to use the explorer

Switch between revenue and margin. Then select each channel and observe how the trend changes. All chart values are also available in a table, along with a downloadable CSV file.

- **Revenue:** sales value during the period.
- **Cost:** a simplified measure of costs associated with those sales.
- **Result:** revenue minus cost.
- **Margin:** result divided by revenue, multiplied by 100.

Six-month margin is calculated from total revenue and total cost. A simple average of monthly margins would give the same weight to months with different sales volumes.

## From observation to investigation

The chart helps frame questions, not prove answers. Did growth come from lower-margin products? Did unit costs increase? Could discounts or changes in the sales mix explain part of the difference?

Investigating would require product, quantity, price and cost details, consistent definitions and comparable periods. A useful dashboard keeps those questions close to the numbers.

## Limitations

These data were designed to demonstrate a relationship between metrics. They exclude taxes, operating expenses, seasonality and cash flow. The result calculated here **should not be called net profit** and does not support conclusions about any real business.

Before celebrating a single metric, check its context and how it is defined.
